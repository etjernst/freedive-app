Winnow Pattern-W Options

Ten variants based on the new reference: sifting as pattern recognition, with a W revealed inside a field of grains. The goal is to make the symbol feel found and intentional rather than like a generic data cluster.

Option 31

Open Grain Wreath

The closest cousin of the reference: an irregular ring of grains around a clear W. The W is solid enough to survive app-icon scale, while the grains give it the sifting idea.

Option 32

Negative W Field

The W is cut out of a dense field instead of drawn on top. This is more distinctive and app-icon friendly, but it feels heavier and more emblematic.

Option 33

Pale Sift

A softer, almost stamped version. The grains become holes in a pale field, so the pattern-recognition idea is there without lots of loose dots around the word.

Option 34

Half-Sift

The grains gather mostly above and to the right, which makes the mark less medallion-like. It feels more like material passing over the W than a static badge.

Option 35

Vessel W

The W becomes a container that catches what matters. This is more abstracted from the reference, but it takes seriously the earlier idea that the doubled W has a vessel shape.

Option 36

Letterpress Tile

A bounded app-icon version: the W and grains sit in a soft square tile. It is less organic, but clearer for favicon, home-screen, and PWA use.

Option 37

Fine-Grain Halo

A lighter, more delicate halo around the W. This is the least chunky of the icon-led options and may pair better with the refined serif wordmark.

Option 38

Split Read

The W itself has two tones, suggesting comparison between plan and actual or noisy signal and clear signal. It is more conceptual, but still readable as a W.

Option 39

Compact Sans

A cleaner, more contemporary version with a sans wordmark. If the serif starts to feel too literary or wellness-adjacent, this keeps the icon concept but changes the tone.

Option 40

Cropped Emblem

A more finished badge: the grains and W are cropped into a circle. It is the most traditional logo of the set, stronger as an icon but less airy than the reference.
